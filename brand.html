Mehrab Studio

Built from brick, lime
and brass.

Mehrab means the arch that faces Makkah, the most carefully made opening in any building. The identity takes its colours from the materials of Gujrat’s own streets: kiln brick, lime plaster, brass fittings and the silt of the Chenab. Its type pairs a sharp Bodoni with drafting-room mono, the way a drawing pairs the elevation with its notes.

Urdu mark · on Bhatta

The mark is a pointed arch drawn as two lines, the way an architect sketches a wall in section, with an eight-point khatam star where the keystone would sit. Keep clear space equal to the arch’s width on every side. Never stretch the mark, outline the wordmark, or set the Urdu name in any face other than a Nastaliq.

Motif

The jali as pattern.

01Use the khatam star as a bullet, a divider or a full jali field. Never as decoration on a photograph.

02Lines are 1px brass on Raat, or 1px Raat on Chuna. No gradients, no drop shadows.

03Corners stay square. Buttons, cards and forms have no rounding, like drafted sheets.

04Italic is the brand’s gesture. Use it once per heading, on the words that turn the sentence.

05A faint paper grain sits over dark pages so screens feel printed, not lit.

Voice

Plain words, local facts.

We say

  • “A 10 marla house, three storeys, four bedrooms.”
  • “The render you approve is the house we build.”
  • “We reply within 24 hours on working days.”
  • Marla, kanal, baithak, sehan, barsati: the words clients use.

We avoid

  • “World-class, iconic, best in Pakistan.”
  • Statistics we cannot source.
  • Stock photos of glass towers that are not ours.
  • Jargon a family would need to look up.
